# Vocal Mastering and Effects Chains

Work non-destructively. Always keep a dry or lightly processed vocal stem.

## Clean Intimate
1. High-pass ~80–100 Hz
2. Gentle de-esser (5–8 kHz)
3. Soft compression (2:1–3:1, low ratio, musical attack)
4. Short plate or small room reverb (10–20% wet)
5. Subtle tape or tube saturation
6. Level into the mix so full track sits around –14 to –16 LUFS integrated

## Radio Polish
1. Tighter compression + parallel/upward compression for density
2. Presence EQ boost 3–5 kHz (careful with sibilance)
3. Controlled de-ess
4. Stereo width on doubles / harmonies only
5. Brickwall or soft limiter for competitive loudness
6. Target often closer to –12 to –14 LUFS depending on genre

## Club / Wet
1. Longer reverb or rhythmic delay throws
2. Sidechain ducking to kick or main rhythm if present
3. More aggressive saturation or mild distortion for character
4. Wider stereo image
5. Ensure mono compatibility for club systems

## Warm Plate / Soul
1. Darker plate reverb (longer decay, rolled-off highs)
2. Gentle tape-style saturation
3. Rounded low-mids (avoid mud)
4. Soft high shelf if needed
5. Preserve dynamics; avoid over-limiting

## Measurement
- Integrated LUFS
- True peak (keep ≤ –1 dBTP for most delivery)
- Short-term loudness for consistency across sections

Use the normalize and analysis scripts after processing. Compare variants (as with the V3A–V3E masters) and choose by ear + metrics.
